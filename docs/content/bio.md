Cross-checked 2026-09-22 across four independent sources: his own site, musicaenmexico.com.mx, inba.gob.mx press bulletins, and Guanajuato state / Cámara de Diputados bulletins. They agreed on every checkable fact. Discrepancies are flagged.

## Facts

**Identity** — Mauricio Náder Schekaibán, b. 1967, Mexico City.

**Start and debut** — began studying and performing at nine; **debut as soloist with the Orquesta Sinfónica Nacional at nineteen**, at the Palacio de Bellas Artes. All four sources agree. His site states performances from 1976 to 2016 total over 800.

**Training** — Conservatorio Nacional de Música → University of Houston School of Music (BM) → Eastman School of Music (MM), where the **Performer's Certificate was awarded unanimously**. Piano teachers: **Carlos Bueno, María Teresa Rodríguez, Nancy Weems, Anton Nel**. Private composition with **Samuel Adler and Brian Zoelch** (his site only, not corroborated).

**Awards**
- **1996 — First Prize, Bartók-Kabalevsky International Piano Competition (USA)**, and the same year the **Commemorative Medal of the Austrian Millennium**.
- First place also at: Bienal de Piano de México, Concurso Nacional Edvard Grieg, Concurso Nacional de Xalapa, Texas Music Teachers' Association, Tuesday Musical Club Young Artists Awards.
- 2008 — "Most Distinguished" mention, Ibla Grand Prize, Italy.
- FONCA fellow, *Intérpretes con trayectoria destacada*.
- His photograph appears on the commemorative stamp *México del Siglo XX al Tercer Milenio / Arte* (2000). His site only, unverified elsewhere.

**Recordings** — **more than thirty** CDs as soloist, chamber musician and vocal accompanist (INBA's current wording, and his /bio-press-notes page).
⚠️ His own /additional-information page still says "over twenty" — that page is older. Use **more than thirty**.
Named albums: *Apuntes para piano*, *Mauricio Náder à Paris*, *Piano Music*, *Recital*; Quintanar's Piano Concerto with the OSUG under Juan Trigos; *Bosque adentro*.
Film work: *Güeros* (Alonso Ruizpalacios), the documentary *El gesto eterno*, and TV commercials.

**Countries — twenty-two.** Mexico, USA, Canada, Germany, Bulgaria, Spain, France, Italy, Norway, Portugal, Belize, Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, Ecuador, Panama, Peru, Puerto Rico, El Salvador, China, Lebanon, Pakistan.
⚠️ The earlier research brief said twenty-one — it was missing Panama. **Twenty-two is correct.**

**Venues** — Jay Pritzker Pavilion (Chicago), Inter-American Development Bank (Washington), Spivey Hall (Atlanta), Americas Society (New York), Fundación Príncipe de Asturias (Spain, 1994 — his international breakthrough), National Centre for the Performing Arts (Beijing), Oslo Konserthus, Hôtel National des Invalides (Paris), Centro Cultural de Belém, Teatro Municipal (Rio), Art Institute of Chicago.

**Current positions — two, both present tense**
1. **Concertista de Bellas Artes (INBAL)**, at the rank of **concertino, the highest level in that group**.
2. **Founder, Academic Director and Piano Professor, Centro Mexicano de Posgrados en Música, Puebla — since 2006.**
⚠️ His English bio contradicts itself on #2, using past tense in one paragraph and present in another. Present tense is correct.

**Premieres**
- **Mexican premiere of George Crumb's *Makrokosmos* Books I and II.**
- Premieres in several countries of the piano concertos of **Manuel M. Ponce** and **José Rolón**.
- World premiere of his own ***Toccata ostinata***, February 2025.

**He composes and arranges.** Own works: *Four Parodies for piano*, *Silencio imaginario*, *Transfiguración sobre el Preludio No. 4 de Chopin*, *Rachmaninoff in Salzburg*, *Toccata ostinata*, plus piano transcriptions (Rachmaninoff's *Vocalise*, Moncayo's *Huapango*, Handel, Minkus). Presented at festivals in Mexico, England and Indonesia.

**Ensembles** — Ensamble 3 (current), The Composer's Forum Ensemble (Rochester), Ensamble Ónix, Ensamble Cello Alterno.

**Jury** — Lang Lang–Telefónica International (2012), Bartók-Kabalevsky (USA), Bucaramanga International Piano Festival (Colombia), Concurso Nacional "Angélica Morales", Manuel de Falla Piano Competition. Also on FONCA and state grant selection committees.
