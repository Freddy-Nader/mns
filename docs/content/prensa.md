# Prensa — verified quotes

**On the landing page:** Life & Style (México) · Houston Chronicle (Estados Unidos) · Braunschweiger Zeitung (Alemania). Three, chosen for geographic spread as much as for wording.

All from https://mauricionader.net/bio-press-notes unless noted, checked 2026-09-22. His site gives publication and year only — **no critic bylines exist for any quote.**

## The three on the landing page

> "Cuando Mauricio Náder toca el piano, despierta una parte insólita del público: el mundo de las pasiones, la sensualidad, la exaltación, la energía y la voluptuosidad."
> — **Life & Style, 2006, México**

Trimmed on the page after "la exaltación" with the sentence closed — the full list of five nouns is too long to read at pull-quote size. The English version on his site ("...energy and voluptuousity", sic) is the translation; we use the Spanish original because it is the original.

> "Today, no other Mexican pianist matches Mauricio Nader's busy concert schedule, precise technique and intense passion."
> — **Houston Chronicle**, United States (no date given)

Kept in English, untranslated, per [ADR-0002](../adr/0002-fully-bilingual-structured-content.md): a quote is reproduced verbatim in its original language on both language versions of the site.

> "From beginning to end, Náder obtained from the piano a pearl-like sound, underlying every phrase with elegance and grace."
> — **Braunschweiger Zeitung, 1999, Alemania**

Also kept in English. Note this is itself a translation — the original review would have been in German, and his site carries only the English version. If the German original can be recovered it should replace this, since the rule is "original language, verbatim".

## "The Indiana Jones of the piano" — do not use as press

> "Maintaining a blistering career of engagements, Mr. Nader has become the Indiana Jones of the Piano World"
> — **Price, Rubin & Partners**, United States

This is **not** a press quote. Price, Rubin & Partners is a US artist-management and PR firm, so this is promotional copy written to sell him. Attributing it to "US press" would be false. It was in the original research brief as a press line and that was wrong.

It can still be used — but only attributed to the firm by name, and not in a section headed *Prensa*. The landing page drops it and leads with the Houston Chronicle instead.
