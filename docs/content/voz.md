# Voz — how the site's copy is written

Set 2026-10-10, after a review against the `copywriting` skill (`.claude/skills/copywriting`, coreyhaines31/marketingskills). It applies to every interface string in `lib/dictionaries/` and to free text in `content/`.

## Impersonal

The site does not narrate Mauricio in the third person, and it does not speak as him. Nobody is the narrator.

- **Nouns and tasks.** Write *Conciertos*, *Fechas publicadas* and *Arreglos para piano*. Don't write *Sus conciertos*, *Sus fechas* or *Su arreglo*.
- **Headings and buttons are tasks in the infinitive**, or a noun. Write *Escuchar en vivo*, *Contratar un concierto*, *Tomar clases*, *Comprar partituras* and *Pedir un arreglo*. Don't write *Escúchalo*, *Aprende con él* or *Llévalo a tu escenario*.
- **No pronouns that point at him**: *él*, *lo*, *le*, *su/sus*, *suyo*, *his*, *him*, *he*. When a sentence needs the person, use the name. For example, *estrenados en México por Mauricio Nader en 2009*.
- **The reader can be addressed** (*tú*, *you*) where they act. Examples: *Describe tu proyecto*, *Tu nombre*, *¿Programas una temporada?*.
- **Messages the visitor sends** (the WhatsApp and email pre-fills) are in the visitor's voice: *Hola, Mauricio. Me interesa tomar clases de piano contigo.*

## Exceptions

- **The biography** (`/bio` and the landing page's short bio) is in the third person, with the subject left implicit wherever Spanish allows. Programme notes and press reprint a biography as written, and that genre is third person by convention.
- **Quotes** are verbatim, whoever wrote them. That includes his own line on `/contacto`.
- **Names** keep his casing and his subtitles: *MAKROpiano*, *pianoffortissimo*, *Contemporary Music — Prepared Piano & Extended Techniques*.

## From the copywriting skill

- **Specific over vague.** Write *28 partituras desde $150 MXN*, not *partituras para todos*. Every claim carries a number, a name or an action, and a number is never invented.
- **No AI tells.** Avoid these:
  - "No es X, es Y" reveals.
  - "Sin X, sin Y" lists.
  - Questions that answer themselves.
  - Colon reveals.
  - Trailing pile-ons of clauses.
  - Em dashes in titles, headings, buttons and short copy. Page titles use a middle dot: *Clases · Mauricio Nader*.
- **One idea per sentence.** Read every sentence over 20 words aloud.
